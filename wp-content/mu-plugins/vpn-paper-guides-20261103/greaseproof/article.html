Greaseproof bakery bags are specified to resist grease from the food they hold. For a wholesale order, describe the pastry, its condition when packed, the expected contact time and the handling route before choosing the paper and bag construction. Check the finished sample with the real product. Paper colour, GSM and the word “greaseproof” are not enough to establish the performance of every bag.

A croissant at the counter, a glazed doughnut in a takeaway order and several pastries carried to another outlet impose different requirements. The useful buying question is which specification keeps that product presentable through that journey, with suitable documentation for the intended food-contact use.

Separate grease, moisture and heat requirements

Grease resistance describes one barrier task. It does not automatically make a bag waterproof, suitable for a steam-heavy product, or safe to put in an oven. If the bakery needs more than oil holdout, list those needs separately so they can be assessed rather than implied.

A pastry can stain the paper without tearing it. Another pack may keep its exterior clean but lose the product texture the bakery wants. A third may fail at a seam rather than through the face of the paper. Look at these observations separately; increasing paper weight does not necessarily solve all three.

Paper manufacturer Metsä distinguishes baking/cooking and food-packing paper families. That distinction is useful when briefing a converter: specify the intended use of the finished bag instead of treating packaging paper and oven paper as interchangeable. This is not a statement that VPN uses a particular mill’s product.

Tell the supplier what the pastry will do to the pack

Do not choose the least demanding product as the only approval sample. If the same bag will hold a dry cookie and a buttery pastry, test both relevant situations. A shared pack may simplify stock, but it should be accepted for the full intended range rather than assumed to work because the dimensions match.

The broader bakery packaging material guide covers choosing between different packaging formats. This article stays with bags and the barrier questions that matter when ordering them.

Ask how the grease resistance is provided

The proposed material may rely on a grease-resistant paper structure, a coating, a liner or a combination. Ask the supplier to identify it. “Brown kraft” and “white paper” describe too little to compare the barrier route or the finished bag’s suitability.

Keep the paper grade and any additional layer on the specification. If the material changes, review the relevant documentation and repeat the affected application checks. Two sheets that look alike may have different intended uses or printing requirements.

Ask what evidence supports any performance figure. If a supplier offers a laboratory oil-resistance result, identify the test method, sample and conditions. Do not translate a laboratory number directly into a guaranteed number of hours with a hot pastry. The food, folds, seams and handling of the finished pack still matter.

Review the complete bag for food contact

Identify the food-contact paper and any coating, film, adhesive or printing arrangement that needs assessment. Request documents for the actual construction and intended conditions, including the food type and the market where it will be sold. A certificate for another material family is not a substitute.

For US orders, the FDA explains that the regulatory status of a food-contact material depends on its components and applicable use conditions. Ask the responsible supplier or compliance adviser to confirm the appropriate basis for the specified bag. Do not treat a general “food grade” sales statement as the whole assessment.

There is a particular reason to check old grease-barrier documents. In January 2025, the FDA announced that 35 food-contact notifications related to certain PFAS grease-proofing uses were no longer effective. This does not certify an alternative bag. For a current purchase, request documentation that actually covers the selected materials and market, including any PFAS-related requirement in your purchasing specification.

This guide does not establish that a VPN material is PFAS-free, certified compostable or authorized for every food use. Those are order-specific questions requiring appropriate evidence.

Trial the finished bag under a written bakery scenario

Set up a simple comparison with your quality and bakery teams. Define the actual pastry, portion count, product condition, packing action, closure, holding period and transport or handover sequence. Use comparable products and conditions when reviewing two bag options.

  1. Identify each candidate bag and its material revision.
  2. Pack the representative product using the normal staff action.
  3. Observe the base, side seam, folds and exterior at the agreed stages.
  4. Note staining, transfer, sticking, tearing and ease of removal separately.
  5. Evaluate the product presentation as well as the empty bag.
  6. Record the result and the reason for accepting or rejecting that application.

This is an application-trial template, not a certified food-safety or shelf-life test. Do not create a favourable demonstration by changing the pastry or observation period between samples. If your team needs a formal barrier or compliance test, agree the method with a competent provider.

Inspect the seam and base after emptying. A clean outer face can hide a failure at a folded joint or damage caused by removing a sticky item. Photograph the relevant area and keep the food and packing conditions beside the image so the observation is meaningful later.

Approve branding on the actual barrier paper

A new paper surface can change how a printed colour or fine detail appears. Use the selected material when reviewing an important logo, rather than assuming a proof on another stock will look identical. Confirm the print side, coverage and position in relation to folds, seams and food-contact areas.

For a small pastry bag, the closing fold can remove much of the visible panel. Place the critical logo and text where staff leave them exposed after packing. If the bag is used in several outlets, review the same packing action at each one instead of assuming that staff will fold it exactly as the artwork designer intended.

Keep the barrier decision and the artwork decision connected. Adding a decorative finish or changing the printed surface should trigger a review of the affected material and use requirements, not just a new photograph.

Compare the whole quotation, including packing and variants

Specify quantity per bag size and artwork. A nominal total of 100,000 bags could represent one long run or several smaller versions with different setup and inventory requirements. Use that number only as an illustrative order size; the actual minimum and production route need confirmation.

Request separate identification of the material, bag format, printing, sample scope and outer packing. Compare bundles and cartons as well as the unit price. A low count per bundle may suit a small counter, while a distributor may prefer a different pack for replenishment. Put the receiving requirement in the brief before production.

Greaseproof bag questions

Is brown kraft paper automatically greaseproof?

No. Ask for the actual paper and barrier specification. Colour and a kraft appearance do not establish resistance to the food’s grease or the suitability of the completed bag.

Can a greaseproof bag go in an oven or microwave?

Do not assume so. Specify any heating use separately and obtain evidence for the complete material, printing and construction under that use. A serving bag and a baking product are different specifications.

Will a window preserve grease resistance?

A window introduces a film, its attachment and additional edges. Review the combined construction and test the relevant contact areas with the intended food. A paper-only result does not verify a windowed bag.

Are greaseproof bakery bags recyclable?

Assess the actual paper, coatings, liners and level of food contamination against the destination’s collection and processing system. Do not print an environmental claim solely because the pack has a paper appearance.
