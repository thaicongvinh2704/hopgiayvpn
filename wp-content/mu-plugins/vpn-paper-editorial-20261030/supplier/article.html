A quote for 100,000 paper bags can describe a very different order from the one your stores actually need. The total may be split across several sizes, printed designs and delivery dates. A supplier that prices one uninterrupted run has not necessarily quoted that programme.

Choose a paper bag supplier for bulk orders by checking whether its written quotation, sample plan and delivery commitments cover the same bag specifications and purchase schedule. Establish those requirements first, then compare price. A low unit price is useful only when you know what it buys.

Turn the headline quantity into a purchase schedule

Separate the quantity you expect to consume from the quantity you are committing to manufacture now. An annual forecast can help a supplier plan, but it does not automatically secure the unit price, storage arrangement or production slot of a firm purchase order.

Ask whether the supplier can combine any stages economically, but keep all three lines visible in the quotation. If a price tier applies only to one size and design, the combined total does not establish eligibility. If deliveries are staged, state who owns the finished stock, where it will be stored and when payment becomes due.

Give every shortlisted supplier the same bag to quote

A photograph is a useful starting point, but it leaves room for different interpretations. One candidate might quote a standard bag decorated after manufacture; another might quote a bag made to your dimensions with printing before conversion. Both can be legitimate options. They are not the same purchasing scope.

Prepare one specification revision for each line in the schedule. Include the packed product, finished dimensions with labelled units, paper requirement, handle construction, reinforcement, artwork version, printed panels, finishes and quantity. Where a choice remains open, ask for a clearly named alternative rather than letting suppliers make silent substitutions.

For example, request “Option A: brown kraft, one exterior spot colour” and “Option B: white kraft, the same artwork and carrying requirement.” Keep the required product fit and performance the same so that you can understand what changes the price. Our paper bag cost guide explains the individual cost drivers; the comparison here is about whether the quotations describe equivalent work.

Also specify how many bags go into a bundle and carton, whether different designs must remain separate, and which labels receiving staff need. For a distributor, a mixed carton can be a fulfilment problem even when every individual bag looks correct.

Confirm who will make and inspect your bags

A manufacturer, converter, wholesaler or trading company may organise a workable supply arrangement. The important question is who controls your specification and who will resolve a problem. Ask which stages the contracting supplier performs and which are placed with partners: printing, bag forming, handle attachment, finishing, inspection and packing.

Identify the legal entity issuing the quotation and purchase contract, then confirm who accepts responsibility for the finished bags. If a partner carries out a production stage, ask how the approved specification reaches that partner and who checks its output. A long equipment list does not settle either question.

A useful capability review follows your proposed construction. For a handled carrier, ask to see comparable handle attachment and reinforcement. For a bakery counter bag, ask about the opening, bottom construction and specified paper. For a window bag, ask how the window material and attachment are controlled. A photograph of a printing press alone cannot answer those questions.

Ask for relevant process evidence, a sample with identified materials and a named contact responsible for the production handoff. If your purchase requires a formal site review, use the broader packaging supplier audit checklist. That audit supports the decision; it does not replace approval of the specific bag.

Use evidence requirements before a price ranking

A simple comparison sheet can separate an acceptable candidate from one that still needs clarification. Use “confirmed,” “open” and “does not meet requirement,” with a document or sample reference beside each result. Avoid awarding a high overall score that hides a failed essential requirement.

  • Product fit: does the proposed construction hold the complete packed item without forcing the opening or distorting the base?
  • Artwork control: is there an agreed print file, colour reference and method for approving changes?
  • Sample relevance: which paper, printing process, handles or assembly steps differ from the planned production?
  • Inspection: what will be checked during production and before dispatch, against which reference, and by whom?
  • Delivery: does the schedule distinguish sample approval, manufacture, dispatch and arrival?
  • Problem resolution: what records are needed to report a mismatch, and how will both parties agree a remedy?

If the bag will contact food directly, request documentation relevant to the proposed materials and actual use in the destination market. A brown appearance or the word “kraft” does not establish food-contact suitability. The FDA explains why component status and intended use matter for food-contact materials in the United States; other destinations need their own review.

Reconcile the quote before comparing the total

First mark each cost as included, separately charged or not yet quoted. Then compare the same purchase quantity and delivery basis. A useful working total is the bag charge plus separately payable setup, samples, packing, inspection and agreed logistics costs. Include duties and taxes only on a consistent basis supported by the relevant logistics or customs advice.

If one quote remains cheaper, ask the supplier to identify the reason: a standard size, a different paper, a simpler finish or another production route. A disclosed alternative can be worth considering. An unexplained difference should remain an open item until you understand it.

Agree how repeat supply will work

A first order and a repeat programme need different planning. Establish how the approved specification, print files and physical reference will be identified for the next run. Ask which changes require notification, such as a replacement paper grade, a different handle component or a new production method.

For a first substantial order, discuss whether a pilot run or a staged release would resolve a specific risk before the full commitment. Confirm its cost and how closely it represents the intended production method. A small decorated stock-bag order does not necessarily demonstrate the custom run you plan to repeat.

For several delivery locations, decide whether the supplier ships one consolidated order or prepares cartons by destination. If you want call-off deliveries, agree stock ownership, storage conditions, release notices and the cost of holding stock. Do not assume that an annual volume forecast creates a warehouse service.

Build the receiving date backwards from the actual approval and logistics steps. A promised production period is not the same as arrival at your warehouse. For orders with a seasonal launch, name the date on which artwork must stop changing and identify who can approve the sample without an internal delay.

Send a brief a supplier can accept or question

Use this compact request to start a comparable discussion:

Please quote the attached paper bag specifications by size and artwork. Show the quantity per specification and delivery batch, paper and handle construction, sample deliverable, setup charges, inspection basis, packing, production period after approval, shipment scope and quote validity. Please list any assumptions or substitutions separately. Our destination is [city and country], and our required receiving date is [date].
