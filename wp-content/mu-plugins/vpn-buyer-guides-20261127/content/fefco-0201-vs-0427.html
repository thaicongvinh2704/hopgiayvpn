If you buy corrugated packaging for export, run a fulfillment line or source boxes for a recurring ecommerce kit, use this guide to decide which structure to sample first. Start with a 0201 for taped transit cases and pallet loads. Start with a 0427 for a shallow kit where the opening presentation matters. Quote both structures when the same product could travel either way. Give suppliers the same pack-out and route so their offers are comparable. FEFCO’s code catalogue identifies the two structures.

Which box fits your shipping route?

  • Palletized wholesale cases: Start with a 0201 when staff load products from above, close cases with tape and stack them in a repeatable pallet pattern. Send the packed case weight, the number of layers and how long cases will remain stacked.
  • A shallow kit sent directly to customers: Sample a 0427 when the lid and presentation are part of the delivery. Check the front tuck after handling and confirm that labels and the security seal can sit on flat surfaces without covering a fold.
  • A fragile or tall item: Trial the product and its insert together. Price a fitted mailer and any outer shipper it needs alongside a 0201 with separate internal protection. Compare which complete pack meets the handling requirements with fewer parts and less movement.

Size the box from the packed product

Measure the largest unit in its actual shipping orientation. Record dividers, protective pads and bags that sit between the product and the box. For example, a product bundle measuring 240 × 160 × 90 mm with a 20 mm protective allowance on each face starts at 280 × 200 × 130 mm inside. That example illustrates the calculation. The actual allowance depends on the material and the fit of a loaded sample.

Put the required inside length, width and depth on the quote brief. Ask for assembled outside dimensions separately when the carton has to fit a pallet, shelf or carrier size bracket. On a mailer, check fit at the folded side walls and front corners. On a slotted carton, check whether the largest pack can pass through the opening without pulling the walls in. See the custom shipping box sizing guide for the full measurement process.

Compare protection and packing cost on the same route

A style code identifies the construction. Board grade, flute, joint, closure, product support and the handling environment all affect performance. For pallet loads, record the loaded weight, stacking height, storage time and humidity. For parcels, describe handling, orientation changes and any fragile surfaces. Ask the supplier what board or compression specification it recommends and how it would be verified on the finished pack.

Time the complete packing cycle with people who will actually use it. Include box erection, loading, inserts, tape or seals and label application. Compare flat storage and inbound packing quantities. A mailer can combine shipping and presentation for a suitable product. A gift pack that needs extra cushioning or scuff protection may still need an outer carton.

Compare board strength and finished-box strength separately

ECT measures the edgewise crush resistance of the board. A box compression test measures how an assembled box resists a compressive load under specified conditions. If stacking is important, agree whether the test will use an empty box or the finished packed unit, and document the load and conditioning agreed with the supplier. See FEFCO's testing methods for the distinction.

Calculate cost per ready-to-ship unit

Compare box price, tooling spread over the quoted quantity, tape or seals, inserts, packing labor and shipment charges on the same order. For example, a 12-second difference at an assumed labor rate of US$9 per hour equals US$0.03 per pack, or US$30 per 1,000 packs. That is an illustration of the calculation. Use your measured time and labor rate.

For parcel quotes, measure the outside dimensions of the closed pack for both samples. A roomier box can increase billable weight on services that use dimensional pricing. Compare both using the carrier's rules for the account and route. UPS explains how dimensional weight is calculated.

Run a side-by-side sample check

  1. Load the heaviest and largest versions in both samples. Record fit and movement after closing.
  2. Check seam closure, tuck engagement, label space and any contact between the pack and the printed surface.
  3. Compare the intended stack and handling route. Use the same contents and protection so the results are comparable.
  4. Log the approved board, dimensions, insert, closure and acceptance criteria against the sample version.

Record the outcome for each style

Use the same row on both sample sheets: packed SKU and weight, time to assemble and seal, material and tape consumed, maximum stack, damage observed and the change required. Record any added outer carton separately. This helps procurement compare a ready-to-ship unit rather than two empty boxes.

Set pass criteria before the trial. Write down the maximum movement you can accept, which marks or damage fail the trial, whether the closure may lift, and what counts as a usable barcode and legible label. Agree how the boxes will be held and handled, including any test protocol the carrier or customer requires. Approve the same board, inserts and closure that will go into production.

Questions buyers ask before requesting a quote

Is 0201 always cheaper than 0427?

Compare the total packed cost at your volumes. Box price, tooling, tape, assembly time, protection and freight can shift the result. Ask both suppliers to quote the same inside size and quantities.

Can I ship a 0427 directly?

Trial the full pack on the intended route. Check lid security, exposed corners, label space and whether a seal or outer carton is needed. Buyers can compare available structures in VPN’s corrugated mailer collection.

Are 0201 and 0427 size codes?

They describe how a box is built and closed. Specify inside length × width × depth separately, along with board, closure and print. For the 0201, FEFCO Recommendation 101 describes how to determine internal dimensions for a flap-type case.

Ask the supplier to return the same four items for both styles

Drawing and sample revision
FEFCO code or documented variant, usable inside size, assembled outside size and how the box closes.
Loaded configuration
Board grade, quantity and orientation of units, insert or pads, tape or seal, and the completed case weight.
Test and acceptance
Agree the stack pattern, handling sequence, sample count and what counts as failure. Record the result against that revision.
Commercial comparison
Separate unit price from tooling, samples, inserts and freight. Use the same quantities and delivery terms.

If a tuck front opens during the handling trial, include the proposed seal in the next trial and quote. If the mailer then needs an outer case, compare that complete combination against the taped carton. Keep the accepted sample or photographs of its packed configuration with the approved drawing.
