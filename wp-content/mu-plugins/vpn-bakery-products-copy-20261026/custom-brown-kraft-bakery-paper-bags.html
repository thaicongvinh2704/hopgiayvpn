Start with the pastry, not a generic bag size

A croissant, a bread roll and a filled pastry may need different bag proportions even when their weights are similar. Measure the product at its widest point and consider how staff will put it into the bag. Leave sufficient room to avoid pressing a delicate top or breaking a flaky edge. If you need several pieces in one bag, describe their arrangement rather than quoting only an estimated total weight.

The side gussets let the bag expand, while the base provides a surface for the contents. Neither feature guarantees that every pastry will sit upright. A wider base may be more useful for multiple items; a taller format may work better for a small loaf or an item handed over vertically. We can discuss different dimensions, but the final size should be checked with the actual product or an accurately measured substitute before approval.

Match the paper to grease, moisture and contact conditions

Brown kraft describes an appearance and material family, not a complete food-packaging specification. A plain paper bag may suit some dry products, while buttery or filled pastries may need a different paper or an additional barrier. Explain whether food touches the bag directly, whether it is wrapped first and whether it is packed warm or at room temperature. These details affect which materials should be considered.

If a grease-resistant option is needed, make it an explicit requirement in the enquiry. Do not assume that the brown paper pictured has that treatment. For direct food contact, request documentation applicable to the selected material and destination market before approving production. The finished bag should be reviewed under the intended packing conditions. A visual comparison with a croissant in a reference image cannot replace material confirmation or a practical test with your own product.

Make bakery branding clear at a busy counter

The broad front panel offers space for a bakery name or simple illustration. A dark single-color mark can suit the brown background, while a more detailed design may need further print review. Artwork that looks clear on a white screen may read differently on kraft paper. Send your logo and any color reference so we can discuss the intended result before preparing the sample.

Place important text away from the base fold and the side gusset creases. If your staff folds the top after filling, reserve that area so the main mark remains visible. A label or date stamp may need its own clear zone. For multiple outlets, consider whether the same bag will serve the entire brand or carry different store information. Recording those variations at the start helps prevent an unnecessary mix of artwork versions within a bulk order.

Choose a counter bag that staff can open and fill

Bakery packaging needs to work during the morning queue, not only in a product photograph. Ask staff to open a sample from a flat stack, load a representative item and close or fold the top as they normally would. Check that the mouth opens easily and that the product does not catch on a seam. If tongs are used, include them in the handling trial.

A handleless bag is intended for a different handover experience from a shopping carrier. It may be held around the body or folded at the top according to the contents. For several wrapped products, a separate carrier could be more appropriate. We can help compare formats without treating one construction as suitable for every order. No specific load rating, heat resistance or holding time is established by these reference views.

For a menu with changing products, test the items most likely to challenge the bag rather than only the easiest one to pack. A broad pastry checks the opening, a taller item checks the usable height, and a richer recipe may reveal a need for additional grease resistance. Record the packing temperature and how long the item normally remains inside. These observations give the material discussion a practical basis and help distinguish a paper selection problem from a size or handling problem before a large run is approved.

Bakery bag buying questions

Are these the same as greaseproof bakery bags?

Not necessarily. The reference shows plain brown paper, and no grease-resistance rating is confirmed. If you pack buttery pastries or other oily products, request a suitable material option and test it with the actual contents before approval.

Can food touch the paper directly?

Direct contact depends on the selected paper, inks, adhesives and intended conditions. State the destination market and food type so the required documentation can be reviewed. Do not infer food-contact suitability from the images.

Yes. Send your artwork, preferred print colors and bag size. We can support the layout and review the logo position against the top fold, side gussets and base seam before making a sample.
