A flat bag serves a different job from a shopping carrier

A handleless flat bag can work for lightweight counter items, stationery, cards, wrapped accessories and selected food applications when an appropriate material is confirmed. It is not a substitute for a handled shopping bag carrying a heavy purchase. Think about how the customer will hold it and whether it will be placed inside another carrier. The intended handover helps determine both the format and the paper specification.

Start with the actual item dimensions, including any wrapper, retail card or protective sleeve. The bag mouth must allow easy loading, and the usable height must account for any top fold. An item with a bulky or irregular profile may need a gusseted construction instead. If your product sits tightly against the seam or stretches the opening, discuss another size rather than assuming that a thicker paper alone will solve the fit problem.

Use the white panel to make small branding legible

The plain surface offers a neutral background for a store name, a restrained pattern or a short service message. Decide which panel faces the customer when the bag is filled. A logo placed centrally on an empty flat bag may appear lower once the top is folded. For narrow bags, a compact mark can be more readable than a long line of small lettering.

Provide your logo in vector format where available, along with any required text and color references. We can support the design layout and prepare it for review. One-color and multicolor work should be discussed against the chosen paper and production quantity. A white reference image does not prove the exact shade, opacity or print result of a final paper grade; review the actual sample if these are important to your brand.

Specify the paper for the contents and handling

Paper selection depends on more than the appearance of the empty bag. A small accessory with a sharp retail card edge may stress a different area from a soft wrapped item. A translucent look may be acceptable for one retailer but unsuitable when the contents should be less visible. Tell us whether opacity, stiffness, surface feel or a particular folding behavior matters to the purchase.

Thickness and grammage can be customized after the application is reviewed. No exact paper weight or load capacity is assigned to the reference images. For food applications, describe direct or indirect contact, grease and moisture exposure, temperature and the time in the bag. Any required barrier or food-contact documentation must be part of the specification. Plain white paper should not be described as greaseproof simply because it looks clean or smooth.

Check the opening, seam and usable dimensions

The top opening needs to separate cleanly so staff can load the bag without tearing an edge. Check a sample using the same handling method used at your counter. If staff works quickly with a stack, it is useful to try several bags in sequence rather than checking only one. This reveals whether the size and opening suit the actual packing task.

The bottom seam also deserves attention. An item should fit without concentrating force against a narrow corner or folded edge. Specify usable dimensions and any required tolerance during sample review. When measuring, distinguish the outside height from the space available after a top fold. The images help show the general construction, but a measured drawing and approved sample provide a better production reference than a photograph alone.

Retailers using the bag for several small items should check the complete group, including any receipt or promotional card. A bag that fits the main product may become awkward when the remaining pieces are added. If privacy is important, inspect the filled sample under the lighting used at your counter and ask about opacity before selecting the paper. For prepacked merchandise, confirm whether the bag is simply an outer presentation layer or must contribute to product protection. That distinction helps keep the specification proportionate to its actual job.

Questions buyers ask about white flat bags

Does “flat” mean the bag has flat paper handles?

No. This reference is a compact handleless bag, not a shopping carrier with flat handles. If you need handles, describe that requirement and we can review a suitable construction from the wider paper bag range.

Can you print our logo on both sides?

We can review printing on the required panels. Send the artwork for each side, intended colors and bag dimensions. The process and layout should be confirmed with the selected paper and production quantity before sampling.

Are the pictured bags suitable for direct food contact?

That is not established by the reference views. Tell us the food, contact conditions and destination market so a suitable specification and required documentation can be reviewed. Do not assume that the plain white material is a confirmed food-contact grade.

Can we choose a different length or width?

Yes. Provide the contents and required usable dimensions. Allow room for loading and any folded top. If the item is bulky, we may discuss a gusseted format rather than simply making the pictured style wider.

Do you offer wholesale pricing for repeat orders?

Yes, we can quote custom bulk and repeat production requirements. Price depends on the paper, dimensions, printing, quantity split, packing and destination. Use the same specification when comparing the first and repeat order quotations.

What should our first enquiry include?

Send your email, intended contents, bag size, paper preferences, artwork, quantity by version and delivery destination. Include any food-contact or opacity requirement. Request a white flat paper bag quotation and our team will help turn those details into a sample brief.
