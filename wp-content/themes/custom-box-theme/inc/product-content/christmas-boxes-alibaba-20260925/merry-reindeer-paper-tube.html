Stacking, storage, and retail display

Round tubes can stand vertically but are not naturally stable when laid on their sides. Think about how the package will be stored, packed into a master carton, displayed on a shelf, or merchandised in a basket. If units are stacked upright, test the bottom and cap under the expected load and measure headspace so the cap does not carry unintended compression. If the retailer uses shelf labels or barcodes, reserve a location on the back or base that remains readable after the wrap is applied.

Calculate master-carton efficiency using the real tube outside diameter and height. Cylinders leave void spaces in a rectangular carton; a tray, partition, or alternating orientation can reduce movement, but can also add material and packing labor. Confirm the count per carton, separation method, gross weight, shipping marks, and pallet pattern. A round display silhouette may stand out at retail, while a square protective carton may ship more efficiently. Compare those trade-offs before committing to a final structure.
