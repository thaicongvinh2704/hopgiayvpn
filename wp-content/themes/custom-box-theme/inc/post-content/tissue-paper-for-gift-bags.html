For a gift bag, tissue paper is not just a filler. It controls the first visual reveal, adds a soft separation layer, and can connect the bag, box, product and brand colors into one presentation system. The right choice depends on more than a favorite color: GSM, opacity, fiber, print coverage, sheet size and the way staff will pack the bag all change the result. This guide gives buyers a practical starting point for specifying tissue paper for gift bags. Use the GSM table to narrow the paper range, then approve color, artwork and presentation with a physical pack trial. GSM is a grammage measurement—not a complete quality score. For the formal measurement method, see ISO 536:2019 for paper and board grammage.

Start with the gift-bag job, not the tissue color

Before requesting samples, define what the tissue must do at the point of sale and during handling. A boutique may need a clean plume above the bag opening. An e-commerce brand may need one sheet to wrap a garment or separate two products. A corporate gifting program may need a repeatable color system across many bag sizes. Those are different jobs, even when the same 20 x 30 inch sheet is used.

If the product is fragile, wet, oily or heavy, treat tissue as one layer in a packaging system. Pair it with a fitted insert, paperboard box, rigid box or another suitable barrier instead of adding more tissue and assuming that more sheets equal protection.

GSM guide: a useful starting range for gift bags

GSM means grams per square metre. A higher GSM generally means more material per area, but it does not by itself tell you caliper, softness, opacity, tear resistance, surface finish or print performance. Two papers with the same GSM can feel different because of fiber furnish, refining, creping, coating and machine settings.

For most gift-bag briefs, start by sampling 17–20 GSM and 24–30 GSM side by side. Use the same sheet size, color, artwork, fold method and packed product. That isolates the effect of paper weight instead of mixing five variables at once. A catalog range is only a starting point; the signed physical sample is the production reference.

How GSM changes presentation without deciding it alone

GSM affects how much the sheet resists gravity and handling, but presentation is a combination of paper behavior and packing method. A lighter sheet can create a soft, airy plume when several sheets are pinched at the center. A heavier sheet may give a fuller color field and a more deliberate fold, but it can also stand too stiffly or make a small bag look crowded.

This is why “the highest GSM is best” is not a reliable purchasing rule. Choose the lightest range that achieves the intended opacity, print appearance, handling tolerance and presentation after the actual packing sequence.

Color selection: build a system around the bag, product and channel

Color works best when specified as a system rather than as an isolated tissue swatch. Compare tissue against the finished bag, the product or inner box, the handle and the lighting in which the bag will be presented. A color that looks balanced on a screen can appear dull, too bright or unexpectedly transparent on colored tissue.

Printing on tissue: coverage and contrast matter more than decoration

Tissue is light and porous. Fine artwork, heavy solid backgrounds and low-contrast logos can behave differently from the same design on a coated box. Ask the supplier to recommend the print process and ink system for the selected paper, then approve a physical proof or production-representative sample.

Presentation methods that survive a real packing line

There is no single correct way to place tissue paper for gift bags. Choose a method that staff can repeat quickly and that matches the product reveal you want.

The controlled plume

Pinch one or more sheets near the center and open the corners above the bag. This works best when sheet size and count are standardized. Record target height above the rim instead of leaving the instruction as “fluff nicely.”

The wrapped reveal

Wrap or fold the tissue around the product so the recipient sees the brand layer before the product. The sheet must close without excessive overlap, but not become a loose mass at the bottom.

The base-and-side buffer

Place tissue at the base or sides to reduce light scuffing and visual clutter between items. This is a presentation aid, not a substitute for a fitted insert or tested protective structure. Run a ten-unit pack trial with the intended product or a weight-accurate dummy. Ask three people to pack the bags using only the written instruction. If plume height, fold direction or sheet count varies widely, the specification is incomplete.

The under-specified detail: sheet count, waste and bag size

Many tissue briefs specify GSM and color but omit the variables that determine consumption. Define sheet dimensions and sheets per finished bag. Then calculate the order quantity: Required sheets = finished bags × sheets per bag + approved trial and production allowance. Do not insert an arbitrary waste percentage. Measure allowance during cutting, packing and quality inspection, because waste depends on sheet format, artwork registration, packing method and the number of SKUs.

When tissue paper is the wrong layer

Tissue is a presentation and light-separation material. It is not automatically a moisture barrier, greaseproof paper, structural insert or compression-resistant cushion. If the product can leak, scratch, break, deform or move significantly, move the protection decision to the correct layer.
    `t
  • Heavy or fragile item: specify a box, tray, divider or insert that controls movement; tissue can finish the presentation.
    • `t
  • Multiple products: use compartments or a fitted insert when contact and impact matter.
    • `t
  • Food contact: confirm the exact grade, ink and intended contact condition with the supplier. Do not infer suitability from the word “paper.”
    • `t
  • Wet or oily product: request a suitable barrier or liner; decorative tissue may stain, weaken or transfer color.
    • `t
  • Export or parcel shipping: test the complete bag, inner box, master carton and handling route. More tissue alone will not solve compression or drop damage.

Three questions to answer before approving a tissue sample

What GSM is best for tissue paper in gift bags?

For many presentation-led programs, 17–22 GSM is a sensible first sample range. Move toward 24–30 GSM when more body, opacity, denser artwork or repeated handling is needed. Base the final choice on a physical comparison using the actual bag and product.

How many tissue sheets should go in one gift bag?

There is no universal number. Set sheet count by bag opening, sheet size, product volume and the intended plume or wrap. Run a repeatable pack trial and document the approved number per bag and SKU.

Can tissue paper protect a fragile gift?

It can separate surfaces and soften minor contact, but it is not structural protection. Use an appropriate box or insert for impact, compression, movement, moisture or food-contact requirements, then use tissue to complete the presentation. Next step: send bag dimensions, product weight and size, target color, tissue sheet size, artwork, quantity by SKU and delivery country. That brief lets a packaging team compare GSM, printing and presentation on the same assumptions instead of quoting a vague “premium tissue paper.”
