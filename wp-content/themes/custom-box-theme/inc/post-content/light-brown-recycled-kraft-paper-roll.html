Two rolls can both be sold as light brown recycled kraft paper and still behave very differently in wrapping, printing, folding, gluing or box production. The color may look almost identical, while recycled-fiber percentage, grammage, thickness, tensile strength, tear resistance, water absorptiveness and roll quality are not. For a B2B buyer, the useful question is therefore not simply “Is this recycled kraft?” It is: what is the verified material specification, what must the paper do in the finished application, and what environmental claims can the finished packaging actually support? This distinction matters because recycled content describes where part of the fibre came from. It does not automatically prove that the finished package is recyclable in every market, nor does a natural light-brown appearance prove recycled content.

Light brown recycled kraft is a specification, not just a color

“Light brown recycled kraft paper roll” is commonly used as a commercial description rather than a complete technical grade. Each part of the phrase tells a buyer something, but none of the terms alone is enough to approve material for production.
Term What it can describe What it does not prove
Light brown The visual shade of the sheet Recycled-fibre percentage, strength, recyclability or print performance
Recycled That recovered material is used in the fibre furnish when supported by documentation That the paper is 100% recycled unless that percentage is specifically documented
Kraft A kraft-style paper family or commercial paper description associated with brown packaging papers A single universal GSM, fibre composition or strength level
Paper roll A continuous web supplied by width, diameter or length and core specification Suitability for every printing, wrapping, bag-making or box-converting line
A procurement specification should therefore convert the commercial name into measurable requirements. At minimum, the buyer should know the intended use, grammage, thickness or caliper when relevant, roll dimensions, recycled-content claim, required mechanical performance and any coating or surface treatment.

Start with the job the paper must perform

Choosing recycled kraft by GSM alone reverses the correct buying sequence. The application should establish the performance requirement first; the supplier can then propose a grammage and paper construction capable of meeting it. Typical applications include protective wrapping, void fill, interleaving, retail wrapping, paper bags, sleeves, labels, corrugated components and paper-box applications. These jobs do not require the same surface, stiffness, strength or converting behaviour.
Application Primary requirement Important checks before approval
Protective wrapping Flexibility plus resistance to tearing during handling Grammage, tear behaviour, roll consistency, dust and edge condition
Void fill Easy crumpling with sufficient bulk Grammage, bulk, stiffness, dispensing performance and roll length
Retail or gift wrapping Appearance, foldability and printability Shade tolerance, surface consistency, cracking, ink result and rub resistance
Paper bags Tensile and tear performance through converting and carrying Machine direction, cross direction, tensile, tear, glue compatibility and fold behaviour
Box wrap or sleeve Stable surface and predictable converting Caliper, smoothness, print result, lamination or adhesive compatibility, crease behaviour
Corrugated liner application Structural contribution to the combined board Correct liner grade, strength data, moisture and corrugating compatibility
This is also why a lightweight wrapping roll should not automatically be specified as the structural board for a folding carton. If the material is intended for a finished paper box, the buyer should first define whether the kraft is the structural substrate, a liner, a decorative wrap or one component in a multi-layer construction. Which specifications should a buyer verify?

A reliable RFQ replaces descriptive words such as “good quality,” “strong” or “eco kraft” with properties that can be checked against a data sheet, certificate of analysis, approved sample or agreed test method.

1. Recycled-content percentage

Ask the supplier to state the percentage of recycled material instead of accepting the word “recycled” by itself. Where the distinction matters to your environmental claim, also ask whether the declared content is post-consumer, pre-consumer or another recognised recovered-material category. The supporting document should identify the material or grade covered by the declaration. A certificate or statement for another mill, another grade or an entire supplier catalogue is not necessarily evidence for the specific roll being purchased.

2. Grammage

Grammage is the mass per unit area of the paper, normally expressed in g/m² or GSM. ISO 536 provides a standard method for determining paper and board grammage. GSM is essential, but it should not be used as a substitute for performance testing. Two papers at the same GSM may have different fibre furnish, density, thickness, strength, surface and moisture behaviour.

3. Thickness or caliper

Thickness influences bulk, stiffness, winding, folding and how the paper feels in a converted package. ISO 534 covers methods for determining paper and board thickness, density and specific volume. This is particularly important when the paper must pass through a converting line or when a designer is trying to fit a wrap, liner or paper component into a tightly controlled box construction.

4. Mechanical strength

The relevant strength measurement depends on the application. A wrapping paper may need adequate tear resistance, while bag or converting applications may require tensile data in both machine direction and cross direction. Burst strength can be useful for appropriate paper grades, while corrugated components require tests suited to their actual material category. ISO 1974 describes the Elmendorf method for paper tearing resistance, while ISO 2758 covers bursting strength for paper within its specified testing scope. Do not approve a recycled kraft roll simply because the supplier says it is “strong.” Ask what property was measured, under which method, in which direction and against what acceptance range.

5. Water absorptiveness and barrier treatment

If exposure to moisture, condensation or wet contents is relevant, ask whether Cobb water absorptiveness is controlled. ISO 535 covers determination of water absorptiveness by the Cobb method. Also identify how any moisture or grease barrier is achieved. A coating can improve package functionality but may change repulpability, recyclability, printability, sealing behaviour or the environmental claim that can safely be made about the finished pack.

6. Shade and surface consistency

Light brown recycled paper can show more natural variation than a tightly controlled white coated board. If color is commercially important, approve a physical master sample and define an acceptable shade range rather than relying on the words “light brown.” For branded packaging, also review whether the surface is smooth enough for the required print method and whether fibre variation, specks or surface texture are visually acceptable.

7. Moisture and dimensional stability

Paper is hygroscopic, so storage and environmental conditions can influence curl, dimensions and converting behaviour. For production-critical applications, moisture condition should be part of supplier control and incoming-material inspection rather than treated as an invisible background variable.

8. Roll construction

A technically acceptable sheet can still create production waste if the roll itself is poorly converted. Confirm:
  • roll width and tolerance;
  • outside diameter or roll length where relevant;
  • core inside diameter;
  • winding direction;
  • maximum acceptable number of splices;
  • splice identification;
  • edge quality;
  • winding hardness and telescoping control;
  • protective wrapping and pallet requirements.
These details become increasingly important when the roll feeds an automated printer, slitter, bag machine or other converting line.

Recycled content, recyclable packaging and environmental impact are different claims

This is the point where many packaging specifications become misleading. A paper can contain recycled fibre without automatically making the finished package recyclable in every collection and recycling system. Conversely, a paper package may be technically recyclable even when it is not made from 100% recycled fibre.
Claim What the claim should answer Evidence to request What not to assume
Contains recycled material How much recovered material is actually present? Percentage declaration, material specification and applicable chain-of-custody documentation That “recycled” automatically means 100% recycled
Recyclable Can the finished package enter an appropriate collection, sorting and recycling process? Package construction, material composition, market-specific recycling guidance and, where needed, recyclability testing That brown paper appearance alone proves recyclability
Fibre sourcing certification What verified sourcing or chain-of-custody claim applies? Valid certificate scope and claim documentation That sourcing certification automatically proves local recyclability
Plastic-free Does the defined package contain plastic components? Bill of materials and component specification That plastic-free automatically means lower total environmental impact
Biodegradable or compostable Under which test conditions and disposal system? Relevant test or certification for the exact claim That recyclable, biodegradable and compostable mean the same thing
Eco-friendly What specific environmental benefit is being claimed? A specific, measurable and substantiated benefit That a broad environmental claim is sufficiently precise by itself
For environmental marketing, precise wording is safer than broad language. Instead of “eco-friendly kraft packaging,” a substantiated statement such as “made with X% recycled fibre,” where X is documented for that material, communicates a specific attribute that a buyer can verify.

Why a recyclable base paper can become a more difficult package to recycle

Recyclability should be assessed at the level of the finished packaging construction, not only the base sheet. Paper recycling normally requires the fibre component to be separated and recovered through processes such as repulping and screening. Additional components can change how efficiently that happens. Examples that should be reviewed include:
  • plastic-film lamination;
  • barrier coatings;
  • wax or grease-resistant treatments;
  • pressure-sensitive labels;
  • large plastic windows;
  • hot-melt or other adhesive systems;
  • metallised layers;
  • foils and decorative components;
  • non-paper inserts or handles.
These components are not automatically unacceptable. Some are necessary for protection, food safety, sealing or product presentation. The engineering question is whether the function can be achieved while keeping non-fibre components compatible with the target recycling process. European paper-industry work illustrates this distinction clearly. Cepi's laboratory test method evaluates paper-based packaging through simulated recycling stages including pulping, screening and sheet formation, while the 4evergreen Recyclability Evaluation Protocol uses laboratory data to assess technical recyclability across different recycling processes. This is much more useful than assuming that a package is recyclable because its largest component looks like kraft paper.

How recycled kraft can change the performance of a paper box

If the roll will eventually become part of a custom box, four areas deserve particular attention: structure, printing, converting and finishing.

Structural performance

Recycled fibre has already passed through one or more fibre-processing cycles. Fibre quality, fibre length distribution, bonding, additives and papermaking conditions therefore matter. This does not mean that recycled paper is automatically weak. It means a buyer should not assume that a recycled grade and a virgin-fibre grade at the same GSM will provide identical stiffness, tear, tensile or compression-related performance. For a finished box, approve the actual board or paper construction against product weight, unsupported panel dimensions, closure design, inserts and distribution conditions.

Printing on a light brown substrate

The brown base color becomes part of the visual result. Light inks and process colors do not reproduce the same way they do on a bright white coated sheet. A brand that needs photographic imagery, pastel colors or tightly controlled light Pantone shades should therefore test the actual substrate before approving artwork. In some designs, the natural kraft color is intentionally allowed to show through. In others, an opaque underprint or a different substrate may be required. VPN's existing material guidance similarly notes that kraft is appropriate for natural and minimalist packaging directions but may not be the first choice when very bright color reproduction or a highly controlled white printing surface is required.

Creasing, folding and cracking

The same paper that looks acceptable while flat may behave differently after scoring, folding or wrapping around a rigid edge. During sample approval, inspect the most demanding areas: narrow folds, small flaps, sharp corners, glued seams and any printed area crossing a crease. The correct answer comes from the actual paper, grain direction, thickness, print layer and finishing combination—not from recycled-content percentage alone.

Adhesion and lamination

A rougher or more absorbent kraft surface may interact differently with inks, coatings and adhesives than a smooth coated board. If a box uses glued wraps, laminated graphics, labels or special finishes, adhesion should be validated on the production material. Adding a film simply to obtain a smoother appearance may improve surface aesthetics while making the final structure more complex from a recycling perspective. That trade-off should be decided intentionally.

What should be written on the RFQ?

An effective recycled kraft paper RFQ can be short, provided the requirements are explicit. Include:
  • finished application;
  • required paper grade or proposed grade;
  • target grammage and tolerance;
  • caliper requirement if critical;
  • recycled-content percentage and documentation required;
  • required mechanical tests and minimum or target values where engineering has established them;
  • surface or print requirement;
  • water or grease resistance if required;
  • roll width and tolerance;
  • core inside diameter;
  • maximum roll diameter or required roll length;
  • winding direction;
  • splice limit and identification requirement;
  • shade approval method;
  • packing and pallet requirement;
  • destination market and environmental claims the brand intends to make;
  • request for a production-representative sample before bulk approval.
For food, cosmetic, pharmaceutical or other regulated applications, add the exact compliance requirement separately. A recycled-content statement is not a substitute for food-contact, chemical, migration or other application-specific compliance documentation.

When is light brown recycled kraft the wrong choice?

Recycled kraft should not be selected simply because the project has a sustainability objective. Another material may be more appropriate when:
  • the artwork requires a bright white base and highly accurate light colors;
  • the package requires a barrier function that the proposed paper construction cannot provide;
  • the product load requires structural performance the proposed grade has not demonstrated;
  • extremely clean or tightly controlled surface appearance is essential;
  • the converting line has not been validated for the proposed paper;
  • the destination market requires a recyclability claim that the finished multi-material construction cannot support;
  • a heavier recycled grade would use substantially more material than an alternative structure that meets the same protection requirement.
The last point is important. Environmental purchasing should not be reduced to maximizing recycled-content percentage. Material quantity, box geometry, product protection, damage prevention, coatings, transport efficiency and end-of-life infrastructure can all affect the practical outcome.

Approve the finished packaging system, not the kraft label

Light brown recycled kraft paper rolls can be a practical material for wrapping, retail presentation and selected paper-packaging structures, but the buying decision should be based on measurable performance rather than appearance. The strongest purchasing sequence is:
  1. define the finished use;
  2. identify the paper's exact role in the package;
  3. specify measurable material and roll properties;
  4. verify the recycled-content claim;
  5. evaluate printing and converting on the real substrate;
  6. review coatings, adhesives and other components at finished-package level;
  7. confirm any recyclability or environmental statement for the destination market;
  8. approve a representative sample before bulk production.
If the recycled kraft material is being selected for a branded folding carton, rigid box, corrugated mailer or another custom structure, a custom packaging box manufacturer can work backwards from product dimensions, weight, artwork, finish requirements and shipping conditions before the material specification is finalized.
