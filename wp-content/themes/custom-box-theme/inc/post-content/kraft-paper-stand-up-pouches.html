A kraft paper stand-up pouch is rarely just a sheet of kraft paper folded into a bag. In most commercial applications, the visible kraft layer provides the natural paper appearance while other layers control heat sealing, moisture resistance, oxygen transmission, aroma retention and mechanical strength. For B2B buyers, the useful question is therefore not simply “What GSM should I choose?” but “What complete pouch structure does the product require?” This distinction matters for coffee, tea, nuts, dried fruit, spices, snacks, supplements, pet treats and other products where the packaging must stand upright, survive filling and transport, and maintain a defined shelf life. A heavier kraft layer may improve stiffness and tactile feel, but it cannot replace a barrier layer when the packed product is sensitive to moisture, oxygen, grease, aroma loss or light.

What Is Actually Inside a Kraft Paper Stand-Up Pouch?

A typical stand-up pouch combines three functional jobs: presentation, protection and sealing. Those jobs may be handled by separate layers or by a specially coated paper designed to perform more than one function.
Layer or Component Main Job Typical Buyer Decision
Kraft paper outer layer Natural appearance, print surface, stiffness and tactile character Brown or white kraft, paper weight, surface smoothness and print result
Barrier layer or coating Controls moisture, oxygen, aroma, grease or light transmission Barrier level should match the product and required shelf life
Sealant layer Allows the pouch edges and top to be heat sealed Seal performance must match the filling and sealing process
Bottom gusset Expands after filling so the pouch can stand on a shelf Gusset dimensions depend on fill volume, product density and pouch width
Zipper and tear notch Improves opening and resealing convenience Useful for multi-use products; not necessary for every single-use pack
Optional window or valve Product visibility or controlled gas release Must be evaluated together with barrier and recycling requirements
This layer-by-layer view prevents a common sourcing mistake: specifying the kraft paper in detail while leaving the functional laminate undefined. Two pouches can look almost identical on a shelf while having very different moisture, oxygen and sealing performance.

Kraft Paper Types, GSM, Printing and B2B Applications

GSM describes the mass of the paper layer in grams per square metre; it does not describe the complete pouch thickness. Commercial kraft pouch structures seen in the market use different paper weights and laminate combinations. The ranges below are practical starting points for supplier discussions rather than universal specifications.
Paper Direction Practical Paper GSM Reference Printing Considerations Common B2B Uses
Light natural kraft outer web About 50–60 GSM Suitable for restrained graphics, dark inks and simple natural-brand designs; print testing is important on absorbent brown surfaces Tea, spices, dried ingredients, sample packs and lightweight dry goods
General-purpose natural kraft About 60–80 GSM Flexographic, gravure or compatible digital printing may be used depending on the converter and laminate; white underprint may be needed for brighter brand colors Coffee, nuts, snacks, granola, pet treats and specialty foods
Heavier kraft presentation layer About 80–100 GSM Provides a more substantial paper feel; artwork should account for the brown substrate and any surface treatment Premium dry goods, organic-positioned products, specialty retail and larger pouch formats
White kraft Often specified in a similar commercial range, subject to converter structure Cleaner base for CMYK graphics, fine text and brighter brand colors than natural brown kraft Supplements, premium food, cosmetics accessories and products requiring a cleaner printed appearance
Functional barrier paper Do not select by GSM alone Printing method depends on coating and paper specification; supplier print compatibility should be confirmed Projects attempting to increase paper share while retaining moisture, grease or water-vapor performance
The important procurement point is that these GSM figures apply to the paper component. A quote should separately state the complete material construction or total gauge because a structure such as kraft paper plus metallized film plus PE behaves very differently from a coated paper-dominant structure even when the visible kraft paper has a similar GSM.

GSM Is Not the Same as Total Pouch Thickness

This is one of the easiest specifications to misunderstand when comparing suppliers. A buyer may receive one quotation showing “80 GSM kraft” and another showing “140 microns total thickness.” Those numbers are measuring different things and cannot be compared directly. The first describes paper mass per unit area. The second describes the thickness of the complete flexible structure. A production-ready RFQ should therefore separate at least four variables:
  • Paper specification: natural or white kraft and paper GSM.
  • Layer construction: paper, coating, film, metallized layer, foil or other functional layers as applicable.
  • Total pouch gauge: where relevant to the supplier's manufacturing specification.
  • Required performance: moisture, oxygen, aroma, grease, light and sealing requirements based on the packed product.
If a supplier answers only with a paper GSM but cannot explain how the completed pouch achieves the required barrier and sealing performance, the specification is still incomplete.

Choose the Barrier Around the Product, Not the Kraft Appearance

The kraft surface is a branding decision. Barrier performance is a product-protection decision. They should be evaluated separately.
Product Situation Important Packaging Risk Barrier Direction to Discuss
Tea, herbs and aromatic dry products Aroma loss, humidity and external odors Moisture and aroma barrier appropriate to the intended shelf life
Coffee beans or ground coffee Oxygen, moisture, aroma and gas generated after roasting Higher barrier structure; evaluate whether a one-way degassing valve is required
Nuts, granola and crisp snacks Moisture uptake, oxidation and loss of texture Moisture and oxygen barrier based on formulation and shelf-life target
Powders and supplements Humidity, clumping, seal contamination and product sensitivity Moisture protection plus seal design appropriate to the powder and filling process
Oily or greasy dry foods Grease migration and surface staining Grease-resistant structure or functional coating
Light-sensitive products Light exposure during storage and retail display Opaque or metallized/foil barrier where justified by product requirements
A clear window changes this equation. It can make a product easier to inspect on the shelf, but the window area may use a different material and can change barrier performance as well as end-of-life sorting. A window should therefore be a functional design decision, not an automatic feature added to every kraft pouch.

Printing on Brown Kraft Requires Different Artwork Decisions

Natural kraft is not a neutral white printing surface. Its brown base becomes part of the final color result. Dark logos, black typography, limited-color graphics and deliberately muted designs can work particularly well because they use the natural substrate instead of trying to hide it. Full-color artwork can also be possible, but the buyer and printer should agree on how substrate color will influence the result. For brighter colors, clean product photography or brand colors that need to remain close to an approved reference, a white kraft surface or an appropriate white ink layer may provide more control. The exact process depends on the converter, press and pouch construction.

Common printing routes

  • Flexographic printing: widely applicable to flexible packaging and suitable for repeat production when the selected substrate and ink system are compatible.
  • Rotogravure printing: commonly used for larger-volume flexible packaging with detailed graphics and repeatable multi-color reproduction.
  • Digital printing: useful where the converter supports shorter runs, multiple SKUs or frequent artwork changes without conventional printing cylinders or plates.
Rather than selecting a printing method from a list, ask for a printed sample on the intended material construction. A digital proof displayed on a white monitor cannot reliably show how the same artwork will appear on natural brown kraft.

Features That Should Be Specified Before Requesting a Quote

“250 g kraft stand-up pouch with zipper” is usually not enough information for an accurate B2B comparison. The same nominal capacity can require different dimensions because coffee beans, powder, nuts and dried fruit have very different bulk densities. A better RFQ includes:
  1. Product: what will be packed and whether it is food, non-food, powder, granular, oily, aromatic or otherwise sensitive.
  2. Net fill weight and product density: do not select pouch dimensions from weight alone.
  3. Finished pouch dimensions: width × height × bottom gusset, preferably confirmed with a filling trial.
  4. Material structure: kraft type and GSM plus the required functional layers or performance target.
  5. Closure: heat-seal top, zipper, tear notch, valve, hang hole or other convenience feature.
  6. Artwork: number of designs/SKUs, print coverage, color references and any white ink requirement.
  7. Performance target: expected shelf life and the relevant moisture, oxygen, grease, aroma or light sensitivity.
  8. Filling method: hand filling, semi-automatic line or automated form/fill/seal process.
  9. Destination market: especially where food-contact, labeling, recovery or recycling claims must follow local requirements.
This specification makes quotations easier to compare because suppliers are pricing approximately the same packaging construction instead of interpreting an incomplete brief in different ways.

When a Kraft Stand-Up Pouch Should Not Work Alone

A stand-up pouch is efficient as a primary pack, but it does not automatically solve every secondary-packaging requirement. For premium gifting, fragile retail sets, multi-unit packs or products that need a larger printable presentation surface, a custom paper box can be used around or alongside the flexible primary pack. The pouch manages product containment while the secondary carton can handle grouping, presentation, additional information panels or physical organization. Retail programs may also need a branded carry-out layer. In that case, custom paper bags with logo can connect the pouch or boxed product to a consistent customer-facing packaging system rather than treating each packaging component as an unrelated purchase.

Does a Kraft Paper Pouch Automatically Mean Recyclable?

No. A kraft appearance alone does not establish recyclability. The completed pouch may contain coatings, sealant layers, metallized films, plastic films, foil, adhesives, zippers or window materials. Those components may be necessary to protect the product, but they can also affect how the package should be collected and processed after use. This is why B2B sustainability claims should be based on the finished material construction and the recovery system in the target market, not on the fact that one visible layer is paper. If recyclability or paper-content claims are commercially important, request the relevant material specification and test or compliance documentation from the pouch supplier before artwork is approved. The same discipline applies to terms such as “plastic-free,” “compostable,” “biodegradable” and “mono-material.” They describe specific material or end-of-life conditions and should not be used as interchangeable marketing terms.

A Practical Selection Rule for B2B Buyers

Start with the product risk, then choose the barrier, then decide how much kraft appearance you want to preserve. If the product is a low-risk dry good with a short distribution cycle, a relatively simple structure may be sufficient. If the product is sensitive to humidity, oxidation, aroma loss, grease or light, the protective layer becomes more important than paper GSM. If sustainability targets are part of the brief, the packaging should then be evaluated as a complete material system rather than simply replacing a brown kraft surface with a heavier one. Before bulk production, approve the filled pouch rather than only an empty flat sample. Check whether it stands correctly, whether the gusset opens consistently, whether the zipper remains usable, whether the top seal area stays clean, whether the printed surface creases acceptably and whether the packed product survives the intended storage and shipping conditions. For projects that combine flexible primary packaging with folding cartons, rigid presentation boxes, paper bags or other secondary paper components, working with a custom packaging box manufacturer can help coordinate the visible packaging system around the product. Send the pouch dimensions, packed weight, product type, secondary-box requirement, artwork and destination market so the paper packaging can be planned around the actual filled pouch rather than an estimated size.
