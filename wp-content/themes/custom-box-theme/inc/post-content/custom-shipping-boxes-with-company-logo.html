Custom shipping boxes with company logo should not be quoted from a logo file and a rough box size alone. For an e-commerce, subscription, wholesale or distribution program, the real cost depends on how the product is packed, which box structure is used, how much surface is printed, the corrugated construction, the number of sizes and artworks, the order quantity for each SKU, and how the empty boxes will be delivered.

The most useful buying process is therefore not “find the cheapest printed box.” It is to create one production-ready specification that every supplier can quote against. This guide shows how to choose the box style, printing route, material, dimensions and artwork, then turn those decisions into a comparable RFQ.

Quick decision: use an RSC-style shipping carton when transport efficiency and straightforward branding are the priority; consider a die-cut mailer when the box is also part of the customer-facing unboxing experience. Keep the box dimensions, board construction, print coverage, quantity by artwork and delivery requirement identical when comparing suppliers.

Choose the Shipping Box Before You Design the Logo Placement

A logo can be printed on many corrugated structures, but the structure determines which panels customers see, how the box is closed, how much board is consumed and how efficiently the empty packaging can be stored and transported.

For most branded shipping projects, the first decision is whether the packaging is mainly a transport carton, a customer-facing mailer, or a more specialized die-cut shipper.

Box style Usually makes sense when Logo strategy Main sourcing trade-off
RSC / slotted shipping carton Protection, warehouse handling and efficient case shipping are the main requirements Simple logo, handling marks, website, SKU or one-to-few-color graphics on selected panels Efficient structure, but less of an unboxing presentation than a die-cut mailer
Corrugated mailer box The shipping box is also part of the customer experience Exterior branding, lid artwork, inside printing, messages or patterns More converting and board area may be involved than with a simple slotted carton
Custom die-cut shipper The product, insert, opening sequence or fulfillment process needs a special structure Artwork follows a product-specific dieline and panel map Tooling, structural development and blank efficiency become more important

If the product will travel mainly through warehouse and wholesale distribution, do not pay for a complex presentation structure merely because it gives you more printable panels. If the shipping box reaches the final customer directly, the opening experience may justify a mailer structure and inside printing.

Match the Printing Method to the Artwork, Board and Run

There is no universal printing method that is cheapest for every custom shipping box. The practical choice depends on the supplier’s equipment, artwork complexity, substrate, print coverage, quantity, number of versions and required visual standard.

Flexographic printing

Flexographic printing is widely used for directly printed corrugated packaging. It is particularly relevant when the design uses a restrained number of colors, solid logo elements, shipping marks or repeated graphics.

The important buying question is not simply, “Is flexo cheaper?” Ask instead:

  • How many print colors are required?
  • Is the design a small logo or large-area coverage?
  • Are separate printing plates or setup charges involved?
  • Will the same artwork repeat over a large run?
  • What print quality can the supplier achieve on the specified liner?

Digital printing

Digital corrugated printing can be useful when a project has shorter runs, multiple artworks, frequent version changes or detailed full-color graphics. Because production economics vary by press and converter, do not use a generic quantity threshold from another supplier as your purchasing rule.

A digitally printed box may be economical for one factory and unsuitable for another. Request pricing for the actual quantity, size and artwork rather than assuming that “digital equals low MOQ.”

Offset or litho-laminated graphics

When a shipping box needs more refined graphics, a printed sheet can be produced separately and laminated to corrugated board. This route can support premium visual requirements, but it introduces additional materials and converting steps.

It should therefore be selected because the graphic requirement justifies it—not simply because “offset is higher quality.”

Buyer rule: send the same artwork to each supplier and ask which production route they recommend. Then compare the quoted route, setup, color system, surface, sample method and finished appearance rather than comparing process names alone.

Print Coverage Can Change the Quote More Than the Logo Itself

Two boxes can both be described as “custom shipping boxes with logo” while representing very different print jobs.

Compare these three specifications:

  1. A black company logo printed on one side of a kraft shipping carton.
  2. A two-color logo plus handling information on four outer panels.
  3. Full-color exterior graphics with printed interior panels and a branded opening message.

The structure may be identical, but the amount of printing, setup, ink coverage and converting can be very different.

When requesting prices, define the coverage explicitly:

  • one panel or multiple panels;
  • outside only or inside and outside;
  • spot colors or process color;
  • small logo or large background coverage;
  • one artwork or multiple artwork versions;
  • special finishes, if any.

This prevents one supplier from pricing a simple one-panel logo while another supplier assumes a full branded wrap.

Choose the Corrugated Surface for Both Protection and Print Appearance

The logo does not print in isolation. The outer liner, corrugated construction and surface color affect the finished appearance.

Natural kraft surface

Kraft provides a natural brown base that works well with simple dark graphics and brand directions that intentionally use the paper color as part of the design. However, the substrate influences how printed colors appear.

If a corporate color must look very close to a controlled reference, do not assume the same artwork will appear identical on brown kraft and a white print surface.

White print-facing surface

A white outer surface provides a more neutral base for color graphics and can make full-color branding easier to control visually.

That does not automatically make white board the correct structural specification. Print surface and transport performance still need to be considered together.

Flute and board construction

E-flute, B-flute and double-wall constructions can all appear in branded corrugated packaging, but a flute letter alone is not a complete strength specification.

Size the Box Around the Packed Product, Not the Product Alone

The most useful dimension for a custom shipping-box RFQ is normally the required inside box size after considering everything that must fit inside the shipper.

Measure the complete pack-out, including:

  • the product;
  • retail packaging, if the shipping box surrounds another box;
  • corrugated or paper inserts;
  • dividers;
  • protective wrapping;
  • accessories;
  • manuals or cards;
  • required clearance.

Then state clearly whether the dimensions in the RFQ are internal or external and use one consistent order such as L × W × H.

Why right-sizing also affects shipping economics

Parcel carriers can rate a shipment according to dimensional weight when the package occupies substantial space relative to its actual weight. That means unnecessary box volume can affect more than the corrugated-board cost.

Do not solve this by making the box as tight as possible. The correct target is the smallest practical package that still protects the product, allows efficient packing and survives the intended distribution route.

For fragile products, the insert system and internal movement may matter more than eliminating the final few millimeters of clearance.

MOQ Is a Production-Economics Question

A common sourcing mistake is to ask for the lowest MOQ before the supplier knows what must be manufactured.

The practical minimum can change with:

  • box dimensions;
  • board specification;
  • box structure;
  • printing route;
  • number of print colors;
  • tooling requirements;
  • quantity per artwork;
  • supplier equipment and production planning.

This creates an important distinction for brands with many SKUs.

Suppose a company expects to buy 10,000 shipping boxes in total. If the order consists of one size and one artwork, the production economics can be very different from 10,000 boxes divided across ten artworks, several dimensions and multiple replenishment dates.

For quoting purposes, always show the quantity per box size and per artwork.

Weak RFQ Better RFQ
10,000 branded shipping boxes Size A: 5,000 pcs / Artwork A
Size B: 2,500 pcs / Artwork B
Size C: 2,500 pcs split between Artwork C1 and C2

This lets the supplier evaluate real setup frequency rather than pricing an unrealistic combined quantity.

Prepare the Artwork After the Structure and Dieline Are Confirmed

Logo artwork should follow the approved box structure, not the other way around.

A shipping-box dieline identifies panels, folds, slots, glue areas, locking tabs and other production geometry. If branding is finalized before the actual dieline is locked, seemingly simple artwork may later cross a fold or land on the wrong visible panel.

Send a clean logo master

Vector logo files are generally preferable because they can be scaled without the same resolution limitations as raster artwork. If the design also contains photographs or raster graphics, provide sufficient source quality for the final print dimensions.

Identify the company color target

If color consistency is commercially important, provide the intended color reference and tell the supplier whether the requirement is a process-color reproduction, a spot-color target or simply a visual brand direction.

Do not assume a color displayed on a monitor will reproduce identically on every corrugated liner.

Map important content to the correct panel

The artwork brief should identify:

  • primary logo panel;
  • secondary branding;
  • website or social information;
  • handling symbols;
  • barcodes or QR codes;
  • shipping-label zones;
  • areas that must remain blank;
  • opening orientation;
  • inside-lid artwork where applicable.

Approve the right type of proof

A digital proof can confirm layout and content, but it cannot always represent the complete behavior of material, print and converting. Projects with color-critical branding, unusual substrates, complex structures or tight product fit may justify a physical prototype or production-representative sample before the full run.

When Should You Request a Sample?

A sample is most valuable when it is used to verify a specific risk.

For example:

  • Structural risk: check product fit, closure and insert performance.
  • Artwork risk: confirm panel orientation and logo placement.
  • Color risk: review the actual printed surface rather than the screen preview alone.
  • Assembly risk: confirm whether warehouse staff can erect and pack the box efficiently.
  • Presentation risk: review the finished unboxing experience before committing to bulk production.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do custom shipping boxes with company logo cost?

There is no reliable universal unit price. Cost changes with dimensions, board construction, box style, printing route, print coverage, tooling, quantity per artwork, packing and freight. Send one complete specification to suppliers before comparing price.

What is the minimum order quantity for custom logo shipping boxes?

MOQ depends on the specific production route and supplier rather than being a fixed property of corrugated board. Box dimensions, structure, artwork count, printing setup and material procurement can all change the economic minimum. Confirm MOQ against the exact project.

Is flexographic or digital printing better for a company logo?

Neither process is universally better. Flexographic printing is widely used for corrugated packaging and can suit straightforward repeated branding, while digital printing can be useful for detailed graphics, multiple versions or runs that fit the supplier’s digital equipment. Ask the factory to recommend the route for your actual artwork and quantity.

Should I use an RSC carton or a mailer box?

Use the structure that matches the shipping job. An RSC is a practical choice when transport, warehouse handling and efficient case packing dominate. A die-cut mailer can make more sense when the shipping container is also a visible part of the e-commerce or subscription-box experience.

Can a company logo be printed on kraft corrugated board?

Yes, but the brown substrate becomes part of the visual result. Simple dark graphics can work effectively on kraft, while color-critical artwork may require a different surface or print strategy. Review a representative proof when brand color accuracy matters.

Do I need a dieline before requesting a quote?

Not necessarily. You can request an initial quote using the product, dimensions, quantity, structure direction and artwork requirement. However, final artwork placement should follow the production dieline approved for the selected structure.

What information should I send for the most accurate quote?

Send the packed product dimensions and weight, required inside box size, structure, corrugated requirement, print surface, artwork, print coverage, quantity per size and artwork, insert requirements, delivery country, packing format and sampling requirement.

Is inside printing worth the additional cost?

Inside printing is most useful when the opening experience contributes to the brand experience or communicates something valuable to the customer. For a transport-focused wholesale carton, it may add cost without providing enough commercial value.
