Custom printed tissue paper looks simple until you try to compare quotations. A lower price per sheet does not necessarily mean a lower-cost specification: two suppliers may be quoting different paper weights, sheet sizes, ink coverage, printing methods, artwork versions, packing formats, or setup charges.

For a useful B2B quotation, define six things before comparing suppliers: the job the tissue must perform, paper weight (GSM), finished sheet size and base color, printing method, artwork coverage and repeat pattern, and quantity per design. Once those variables are aligned, MOQ and cost become much easier to evaluate.

This guide explains how to build that specification for retail, fashion, cosmetics, gifts and ecommerce packaging without treating one GSM, one MOQ or one printing process as universally “best.”

Start With the Job: Wrap, Liner or Presentation Layer?

Before choosing GSM or printing, decide what the tissue paper actually does in the package. Custom tissue can serve several visual and handling functions, but those functions do not require the same specification.

Use What the Buyer Needs Specification Priority
Product wrap A sheet that folds around apparel, cosmetics, accessories or gifts Drape, fold behavior, size, logo visibility
Box liner A branded layer between the box and product Sheet coverage, paper color, print contrast, fold position
Retail presentation A visible branded reveal when a customer opens a bag or box Color, repeat pattern, logo scale, print consistency
Interleaving A lightweight separator between products or surfaces Low bulk, appropriate material, sheet size

Tissue paper should not automatically be treated as structural protective packaging. If the product needs impact protection, moisture barrier performance or another measurable protective function, specify that requirement separately and use the appropriate packaging component.

How to Choose Tissue Paper GSM

GSM means grams per square metre. Higher GSM generally means more paper mass per unit area, but it should not be treated as a quality score. A lightweight sheet may be exactly right for soft wrapping, while a heavier sheet may be preferable when the design needs more body or reduced transparency.

Current supplier programs vary considerably, but commonly offered custom tissue weights include lightweight grades around 17–18 GSM and heavier options around 28 GSM or above. Some manufacturers also offer intermediate weights.

GSM Direction Typical Visual Behavior Useful When Buyer Watch-Out
Approximately 17–18 GSM Very light, soft and relatively translucent Classic gift tissue, apparel wrapping, light repeat-logo designs The product or lower layers can influence the visible color; print can show through the reverse side
Approximately 20–22 GSM where available More body while retaining a tissue-like feel Brands wanting a balance between soft wrapping and stronger visual presentation Availability and exact performance depend on the supplier's paper program
Approximately 28–33 GSM Heavier, more structured and less translucent Denser artwork, stronger folds or a more substantial presentation layer Higher GSM increases paper use and may feel closer to lightweight wrapping paper

The best comparison is therefore not “17 GSM versus 28 GSM” in isolation. Ask which paper produces the required appearance after it is printed, folded around the actual product and placed inside the final packaging.

Paper Color Is Part of the Printed Color

A screen preview shows artwork on a backlit display. Tissue paper does not behave like a screen or an opaque coated carton. Lightweight tissue is often semi-transparent, so the substrate color, product underneath it and overlapping folds can all affect the final appearance.

A dark logo on white or pale tissue usually creates stronger visual contrast than a pale ink printed on a dark tissue base. White or light inks can also appear less opaque than expected on colored tissue, depending on the paper and ink system.

For color-sensitive projects, define both variables separately:

  • Base paper color: white, kraft, stock colored tissue or another approved paper.
  • Printed ink color: Pantone reference, CMYK artwork or another supplier-supported color system.

A digital proof is useful for checking position, repeat direction and artwork content. When physical color is critical, however, evaluate a printed sample or suitable production reference on the intended paper rather than approving color from a monitor alone.

Digital, Flexographic, Offset or Screen Printing?

There is no single printing method that is automatically correct for all custom printed tissue paper. The practical choice depends on the supplier's equipment, paper, quantity, number of colors, coverage and artwork.

Printing Method Where It Can Make Sense Main Advantage What to Confirm
Digital printing Shorter runs, test orders, seasonal versions or complex/full-color artwork when supported No conventional printing plates and flexible short-run production Available paper weights, printable area, color reproduction, maximum coverage and unit cost at higher quantities
Flexographic printing Repeat logos, spot-color patterns and larger production runs Well suited to continuous paper-web printing and repeat production Plate cost per color, minimum line/detail limits, ink system, number of colors and quantity per design
Offset printing Supplier programs configured for detailed or multi-color printed tissue Can provide detailed reproduction and controlled process/spot-color printing Whether the specific press and tissue stock are compatible, GSM restrictions, coverage limits and setup economics
Screen printing Specialty supplier-specific projects Can support particular spot-color or specialty applications Do not assume availability; confirm paper, artwork, registration, quantity and cost with the actual producer

Do not select a printing method from a generic “best method” chart alone. Two factories can quote the same artwork using different equipment and still produce commercially valid solutions.

The more useful RFQ question is: “For this paper, artwork coverage, color requirement and quantity per design, which production route do you recommend, and what changes if the quantity doubles?”

Ink Coverage Can Change the Production Route and Price

Ink coverage is one of the most frequently omitted RFQ inputs. A small repeating logo and a nearly solid background may both use one ink color, but they do not represent the same printing requirement.

Suppliers also define coverage differently. Some published programs price standard printing only below a specific percentage of sheet coverage, while others separate light logo printing from full-background printing or require a different paper weight for heavier coverage.

For quotation purposes, describe the artwork as one of the following and send the actual file:

Coverage Direction Example What the Supplier Needs to Check
Sparse repeat Small logos with large unprinted gaps Repeat spacing, logo size, plate/layout efficiency
Medium repeat Denser brand pattern across the sheet Approximate percentage coverage and ink requirement
Large graphic One oversized logo or illustration Placement, printable area and fold visibility
Full or near-full background Most of the sheet carries ink Paper weight, ink load, production route, drying and handling feasibility

Simply writing “one-color printing” is therefore not enough for a reliable quotation.

Build the Logo Repeat Around How the Sheet Will Be Folded

“Repeat our logo across the tissue” is not a finished artwork specification. A repeat pattern has geometry, and that geometry determines what the customer sees after the paper is folded.

At minimum, define four items:

  1. Printed logo size: the final width or height of each logo on the sheet.
  2. Repeat spacing: the horizontal and vertical pitch or intended gap between logos.
  3. Direction: straight rows, staggered rows, diagonal layout or another pattern.
  4. Orientation: whether all logos face the same direction or alternate through the repeat.

The design should then be tested in the intended fold. A technically correct repeat can still perform poorly if the most visible panel after folding contains only partial logos or large empty areas.

MOQ Is a Production-Program Variable, Not a Tissue-Paper Constant

There is no universal MOQ for custom printed tissue paper. Current suppliers publicly offer programs ranging from very small digital quantities to hundreds or thousands of sheets per design, while some industrial programs begin much higher.

This variation is useful information for buyers: MOQ is largely a consequence of the selected production route and specification rather than an intrinsic property of tissue paper.

MOQ can change when you change:

  • printing process;
  • sheet size;
  • paper weight;
  • paper color;
  • number of print colors;
  • artwork version;
  • custom paper or stock paper;
  • packing requirement.

One of the most important RFQ questions is whether the MOQ applies to the whole order or to each unique combination of size, paper color and artwork.

For example, ordering 10,000 sheets split across ten different designs is not necessarily commercially equivalent to ordering 10,000 sheets of one design. Every additional version can add setup, file control, production changes, counting and inventory complexity.

How to Compare the Real Cost of Custom Printed Tissue Paper

A public “price per sheet” is useful only when the specification behind that price is known. Published prices in the market can represent different sheet areas, GSM, printing methods, quantities, countries and freight assumptions.

A better procurement metric is:

Landed cost per 1,000 usable sheets = total applicable production, setup, packing and delivery cost ÷ usable delivered quantity × 1,000.

For repeat orders, separate one-time setup or tooling charges from recurring production costs so that the first order and reorder economics are not confused.

Cost Driver Why It Matters How to Control It
GSM Changes the amount of paper used Do not choose heavier paper unless the presentation or print requirement needs it
Sheet dimensions A larger sheet uses more material Test the actual wrapping pattern before specifying oversized sheets
Paper color Stock white, kraft and specialty/custom colors may follow different programs Ask which stock colors can achieve the intended look before requesting a custom base color
Number of colors Can add print stations, plates, setup or production complexity Use the paper color as part of the design when appropriate
Ink coverage A sparse logo repeat and a full background are not equivalent print jobs Send artwork and request the supplier's coverage classification
Quantity per artwork Setup is divided across fewer or more sheets Consolidate artwork versions when commercially practical
Proof or physical sample Adds approval cost and time but may reduce production risk Use physical proofing where color, material or handling is critical
Packing and freight Flat paper occupies carton and pallet space even though each sheet is light Compare carton count, sheets per pack and delivery terms, not ex-factory unit price alone

Artwork Checklist Before You Request a Quote

A production-ready inquiry saves more time than asking suppliers to estimate from a logo screenshot.

Prepare:

  • finished sheet dimensions;
  • preferred GSM or intended wrapping use;
  • base tissue color;
  • quantity for each artwork version;
  • vector logo or editable artwork where available;
  • Pantone references or other required color information;
  • desired printed logo size;
  • repeat direction and spacing;
  • approximate artwork coverage;
  • one-sided or two-sided printing requirement;
  • product and packaging reference photos;
  • delivery country and required arrival window.

AI, editable PDF or EPS files are commonly easier for printers to scale and build into repeating patterns than low-resolution screenshots. However, use the exact file requirements and template supplied by the selected printer.

Also request the printer's own minimum line thickness, minimum text size, bleed and safe-area requirements rather than copying artwork limits from an unrelated supplier.

Approve the Tissue With the Actual Packaging

Tissue paper does not exist by itself in the finished customer experience. Review the sample inside the real box or bag and around the actual product.

Check:

  • paper feel and transparency;
  • logo visibility over the product;
  • ink-to-paper contrast;
  • repeat density after folding;
  • visible partial logos at fold edges;
  • normal handling, rubbing and creasing appearance;
  • coordination with the box, bag, sticker, ribbon or insert;
  • flat-sheet packing quality and edge condition.

If tissue is being developed as part of a complete branded presentation, coordinate its colors with the custom paper boxes and, when relevant, custom paper bags. Do not assume that the same digital color value will reproduce identically across tissue, coated box paper and kraft paper.

What to Send for an Accurate Custom Tissue Paper RFQ

A strong RFQ can be surprisingly short when the important fields are present. Send the supplier:

Input Example of What to Specify
Application Fashion box liner, cosmetic gift wrap, retail bag tissue, ecommerce unboxing
Sheet size Finished width × length
Paper Target GSM, white/kraft/colored, special requirements if any
Artwork Logo/pattern file plus desired repeat direction
Printing Number of colors, Pantone/CMYK requirement, approximate coverage
Quantity Sheets required for each size and artwork
Approval Digital proof, physical sample or production reference required
Packing Flat sheets, sheets per pack, carton labeling requirements
Delivery Country, city/postal code and required delivery date

When custom tissue is being planned together with boxes, bags or other paper packaging, the same RFQ can also define how the colors and unboxing sequence should work across components. A custom packaging boxes manufacturer can then evaluate the project from the complete packaging brief rather than from the tissue artwork alone.

Custom Printed Tissue Paper FAQ

What GSM is best for custom printed tissue paper?

There is no universal best GSM. Lightweight tissue around 17–18 GSM provides the classic soft, translucent presentation, while heavier grades provide more body and lower transparency. Choose after considering the wrapping method, print coverage, desired feel and supplier's available paper program.

What is the typical MOQ for custom printed tissue paper?

MOQ varies dramatically by supplier and production route. Current market programs range from very small digital runs to industrial minimums in the thousands of sheets. Always confirm whether MOQ is calculated per design, paper color, GSM and sheet size.

Is digital or flexographic printing better for tissue paper?

Neither is universally better. Digital printing can be useful for short runs or complex color artwork when supported. Flexographic printing can be efficient for repeating spot-color designs and larger runs. Compare the methods using your actual artwork, quantity and paper specification.

Can tissue paper be printed with offset printing?

Yes, some suppliers operate custom tissue programs using offset printing. Others use flexographic or digital production. Availability depends on the supplier's equipment and the tissue stock being printed.

Can I print a full background instead of a repeating logo?

Often yes, but full-background coverage should be treated as a different production requirement from a sparse repeat logo. It may affect the suitable GSM, print method, ink requirement, MOQ and price. Send the artwork before requesting a final quotation.

Can white ink be printed on colored tissue?

Some suppliers offer white ink on dark or colored tissue. Because thin tissue and ink opacity can affect the visible result, approve a representative sample when white-on-color contrast is important.

Can both sides of tissue paper be printed?

Some production routes can support two-sided printing, but many commercial tissue programs are one-sided. Lightweight paper can also allow the printed image to remain visible through the reverse. Confirm whether a second printed side creates enough visual value to justify the extra production complexity.

Is custom printed tissue paper automatically food-safe?

No. Do not assume ordinary branded tissue is suitable for direct food contact. If the paper will touch food, specify the exact contact conditions, destination market and required compliance documentation, then use a material and ink system approved for that application.

What artwork should I send?

Send vector artwork such as AI, editable PDF or EPS when possible, together with the required logo size, color references, repeat direction and sheet dimensions. Follow the selected printer's final artwork template, bleed, safe-area, line-thickness and font requirements.

Build the Specification Before Comparing the Price

The most reliable custom printed tissue paper purchase starts with the specification, not with the cheapest unit price. Lock the application, GSM, base color, sheet size, printing route, coverage, repeat geometry and quantity per design. Then compare supplier quotations using the same assumptions.

If you are developing tissue as part of a coordinated box, bag or branded paper-packaging project, send your logo, required tissue sheet size, product or box dimensions, quantity and delivery destination. The packaging specification can then be reviewed before you commit to a production route or compare final quotations.
