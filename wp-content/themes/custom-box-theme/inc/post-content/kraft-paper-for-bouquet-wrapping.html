For bouquet wrapping, choosing kraft paper is not simply a matter of picking brown paper at a certain thickness. A florist wrapping fresh roses has different requirements from a gift brand wrapping dried flowers, and a paper that looks attractive when flat can become difficult to fold, tear around a heavy bouquet, or lose its appearance when exposed to moisture from fresh stems. For many outer bouquet wraps, roughly 60–100 GSM is a useful sourcing range to investigate first, but it is not a universal specification. Lighter kraft can create softer folds, while heavier kraft gives the bouquet more body. The final choice should also consider paper grade, fibre composition, stiffness, surface smoothness, moisture exposure, printing and the way the bouquet will actually be assembled.

Choose Kraft Paper Around the Bouquet, Not GSM Alone

GSM means grams per square metre and describes paper grammage. It is useful when comparing materials, but two kraft papers with the same GSM can behave differently because grammage does not describe every performance characteristic of the sheet. A practical bouquet-wrap specification should consider at least five things together:
  • the size and weight of the finished bouquet;
  • whether the stems or inner hydration material may expose the wrap to moisture;
  • how sharply or softly the florist needs to fold the paper;
  • whether the paper will remain natural or carry printed branding;
  • whether the wrap is a presentation layer or also needs to provide structural support.
This distinction matters in B2B sourcing. Ordering a paper only as “80 GSM brown kraft,” for example, leaves important variables undefined. Surface, stiffness, wet-strength requirement, shade and print method can change how that 80 GSM sheet performs at the wrapping table.

Why Two Papers with the Same GSM Can Wrap Differently

One of the easiest procurement mistakes is treating higher GSM as automatically better. A heavier sheet usually adds mass and often adds body, but bouquet wrapping depends on more than weight per square metre.

Stiffness changes the shape of the bouquet

A paper that is too soft may collapse around a large arrangement. A paper that is too stiff can fight against the florist during folding and create large angular creases instead of controlled layers. The preferred behaviour depends on the presentation style.

Surface affects both touch and printing

Natural kraft can have a visibly fibrous, slightly rough appearance. Smoother kraft grades can accept finer graphics more cleanly. Buyers planning detailed branding should therefore specify the required visual surface rather than approving a material from GSM alone.

Fibre composition can change converting behaviour

Virgin-fibre, recycled-content and blended kraft papers may differ in formation, shade, stiffness and tear behaviour. This does not mean one category is automatically superior. It means a B2B specification should define the required performance and approve the actual paper rather than relying only on a generic material name.

Grain direction can influence folding

Paper has a manufacturing direction. When a bouquet wrap uses repeated folds or is converted into a more structured cone, the relationship between paper direction and the fold pattern can affect handling. For standardized high-volume wrapping, it is worth testing the production sheet in the same orientation that florists will actually use. Buyers who want a broader comparison of kraft, coated papers and paperboard can also review this guide to paper materials for custom paper boxes. The end use is different, but the same principle applies: paper selection should be based on structure, print requirements and use conditions rather than material name alone.

Fresh Flowers Add a Moisture Requirement

Untreated kraft paper should not be treated as waterproof. Fresh stems, hydration packs, wet tissue or condensation can create local moisture exposure, especially near the lower part of a bouquet. There are several ways to address this, and they should not be confused with one another.
  • Keep raw kraft as the outer presentation layer. A separate inner moisture-management layer can prevent the paper from directly contacting wet stem material.
  • Specify a wet-strength kraft grade. This is relevant when greater sheet integrity under damp conditions is required, although the actual performance should still be tested.
  • Use a coated or barrier-treated paper where necessary. The coating system should be defined rather than using the vague word “waterproof.”
Barrier treatments can change the feel, print behaviour and end-of-life characteristics of the wrap. If recyclability is part of the marketing brief, the complete material construction should be verified before making a claim. A paper base does not automatically make every coated or laminated wrap recyclable in every collection system.

Printing on Brown Kraft and White Kraft Produces Different Results

Kraft paper can carry strong branding, but artwork should be designed around the substrate instead of treating kraft like white coated paper.

Natural brown kraft works best when the substrate is part of the design

Black, dark green, burgundy and other strong tones can work effectively as logos, repeating patterns or limited-color artwork on natural kraft. The brown base influences any ink printed over it, so bright brand colors may appear more subdued than they do on a white proof or monitor. When precise light colors are critical, the printer may need a suitable white underbase or another production approach. That capability should be confirmed with the wrapping-paper converter before artwork is finalized.

White kraft gives artwork a more neutral base

White kraft is useful when the buyer wants a paper feel but also needs cleaner color contrast. It can be a better starting point for floral illustrations, seasonal graphics, modern branding and broader color palettes. Color approval should still be based on the production substrate. A digital screen proof does not reproduce the texture, absorbency or underlying shade of the real kraft sheet.

Before bulk production, the most useful sample is not simply a flat printed sheet. Fold it around a representative bouquet. Check whether the corners crack, whether the wrap holds the intended shape, whether damp areas distort the paper, whether printed surfaces rub against one another and whether staff can complete the wrapping process efficiently. This physical-use test often reveals issues that are invisible in a specification sheet.

Connect Bouquet Wrap with the Rest of the Floral Packaging System

Bouquet wrapping is often only one part of the customer-facing package. Flower shops, gifting companies and event suppliers may also need carrying bags, boxed floral gifts, tags or other paper components that follow the same visual direction. For retail carry-out, a bouquet or boxed floral gift can be paired with custom paper bags with logo. Bag dimensions, gusset depth, handle position and reinforcement should be planned around the actual packed item instead of choosing a generic shopping-bag size. Gift campaigns may also combine flowers with candles, cosmetics, confectionery or other products inside a paper box. In that situation, the bouquet wrap, bag and box do not necessarily need to use identical materials. They need a coordinated color, print and finish strategy while each component remains structurally suitable for its own job. If your project extends beyond wrapping paper into custom paper bags, gift boxes or branded paper packaging, a custom packaging box manufacturer can help translate the broader retail presentation into production-ready dimensions, materials, artwork and samples.

Specify Performance First, Then Choose the Look

The most useful way to source kraft paper for bouquet wrapping is to work from the bouquet outward. Define whether the flowers are fresh or dry, how much structure the wrap needs, how it will be folded, the expected moisture exposure and the printing requirement. GSM then becomes one part of a complete material decision rather than the entire specification. For a new B2B project, prepare the bouquet dimensions, preferred kraft appearance, approximate GSM direction, artwork, moisture requirement and intended wrapping method before requesting samples. If the floral presentation also requires custom bags or paper boxes, include those dimensions at the same stage so the complete packaging system can be planned around the finished product.
