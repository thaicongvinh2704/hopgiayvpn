A magnetic closure box sample should close cleanly with the full product set inside. Before releasing a bulk order, check the lid with the box empty, with the insert installed, and with every item packed. Then match the finished sample to the approved artwork, wrap, closure and packing specification. If the lid sits flush when empty but lifts when packed, check the space above the product before asking for stronger magnets.

This checklist is for buyers sourcing book-style rigid boxes for fragrance sets, skincare kits, jewelry and other branded gifts. It covers sample approval and the handover to production. The paper box quality control checklist covers the wider inspection process for the production lot.

Choose the sample that answers your question

Ask the supplier which materials and settings the sample represents. A plain build can settle fit early if the board, hinge, closure and insert match the proposed construction. A flat proof can confirm artwork placement and spelling. Review color and finishes on the wrapped box when the final appearance depends on the actual paper, printing method or foil position near an edge.

Put any temporary sample material in the approval record. For example, if a prototype tray uses a substitute lining, mark the tray geometry as approved and leave finished fit and appearance open until the correct lining is available.

Troubleshoot the lid in three passes

  1. Empty shell. Close the lid normally on a level bench without pushing down on the center. Compare the front corners and check whether the flap meets the front wall squarely. Repeat the opening and closing action and look for hinge spring-back, a twisted lid, or a flap that only catches on one side.
  2. Insert only. Seat the empty insert exactly as it will be fitted in production. Close the lid again. A gap that appears at this stage points you toward the insert height, the way it sits against the liner, or an edge that interferes with the closing path.
  3. Fully packed. Load the real production items in the agreed orientation, including cards, ribbons and accessories. Stop if the lid needs pressure to close. Look for a high cap, a folded ribbon or a raised item. Reopen the box and check for contact marks, product movement and whether each item can be removed without gripping a fragile part.

If the lid is already misaligned on the empty shell, ask the supplier to check the hinge, flap geometry and the alignment of the hidden closure parts. If the gap appears only after loading, record what changed and which item sits highest. Check variants separately where caps, accessories or inserts differ. Size changes that look minor on an artwork file can change the way an assembled kit closes.

Take a fragrance kit as an example. Its flap sits flush with an empty tray but lifts when the bottle and an instruction card are added. Reseating the bottle and trying the card in its own recess isolates their effect one at a time. If the card is the cause, revise its position and repeat the fully packed check before signing off the closure. Record the final layout in a photo so the packing team can reproduce it.

Check the finish with the box closed and open

Start with the closed lid, front flap and the inside of the lid. These are the areas a customer sees first. Compare color under agreed lighting. Tilt the box to inspect matte scuffs, foil gaps near corners and raised areas in the wrap. Check the cut line and lid crease against the finished sample, especially where foil or dark ink crosses a fold. For an appearance issue near the acceptance limit, agree on a reference sample or close-up with a scale and consistent lighting.

Where opening feel matters, agree on a repeatable check. Use the same pull point and direction each time. If your team specifies an opening-force range, define the method and acceptable range for that box. Pull location, flap geometry and material thickness can all affect what a customer feels. Send a written sign-off the production team can use

Date the approval and give the finished sample a reference ID. Share the same reference with procurement, the factory and anyone inspecting the shipment. For orders with multiple variants, make it clear which approvals apply to each SKU.

  • Specify the build. Include the dieline revision, box size and measurement points, board, wrap, liner and insert references. Specify whether dimensions are internal or external and use one controlling unit on the drawing.
  • Record the functional checks. Include loading order, product orientation, photos of the full kit and the agreed closure and removal checks.
  • Set the appearance reference. Include approved artwork and finish references, agreed viewing conditions and any visible limits that are hard to describe in writing.
  • Confirm the packing method. Record units per master carton, orientation, protection between finished boxes and the transit check required for the actual shipping route.
  • Record the release decision. Mark which details you approved, which are still open and which changes require you to review a new sample.

Have the first production units checked against the signed sample before committing the rest of the run. The approved sample sets the reference. The lot inspection checks units drawn from production under the agreed sampling plan. If you use an AQL-based plan, agree on the defect definitions and inspection method ahead of time. For a fragile kit or parcel route, evaluate the retail box inside its intended protective shipper with the product packed as it will be sent. Questions buyers ask before signing off

Can I approve a magnetic box sample from photos?

Photos are useful for reviewing artwork placement and visible changes. If fit, opening feel or product removal is at stake, ask for a physical sample and try it with the full product set. A video can show the opening sequence and give both teams a record of the comparison.

Does a lid that lifts always need stronger magnets?

Start by finding when it lifts. If it happens with an empty shell, check how the hinge, flap and closure line up. If it starts with the insert or loaded kit, look for interference before changing the closure. Retest after each revision so you know which change solved it.

When should I ask for a new sample on a repeat order?

Review a new sample when a change can affect fit, appearance or opening. Common triggers include a new product size, insert or board, a different wrap or finish, or revised hinge and closure components. For an unchanged order, confirm the retained reference is still usable and agree on which first units to check.
