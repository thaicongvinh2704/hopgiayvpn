SBS paperboard stands for solid bleached sulfate paperboard, a white paperboard made from bleached virgin chemical pulp and widely used for printed packaging. For a custom folding carton, however, asking for “SBS” alone is not a complete material specification. Buyers also need to define the coated surface, caliper, stiffness, print requirements and conditions in which the finished carton will be used.

This guide explains how to choose between C1S and C2S, interpret thickness in points and millimetres, compare SBS vs FBB, and prepare a material brief that a packaging manufacturer can assess before quoting or sampling.

What Is SBS Paperboard?

SBS (solid bleached sulfate) is a paperboard grade manufactured using bleached virgin chemical fibre through the board. Its clean white appearance, including the cut edge, makes it useful for cartons where presentation and fine printed details matter. A mineral-coated print surface may be applied to one or both faces, depending on the grade.

Material producer Smurfit Westrock describes SBS as a paperboard family serving premium consumer packaging as well as selected foodservice and healthcare applications. Those application categories do not mean every SBS grade has the same barrier properties, food-contact approval or converting performance.

A folding carton is printed, creased, die-cut and often glued. Coating affects print; caliper, stiffness and grain direction affect folding and closure. A white sheet can still fail by cracking at scores or deforming around its contents.

Practical starting rule: specify SBS as a material family, then approve a particular mill grade and finished carton—not the term “white paperboard” on its own.

C1S vs C2S SBS Paperboard

C1S means coated one side; C2S means coated two sides. Both may be made from white SBS fibre. The important difference is the coating and print behaviour of the reverse face, not whether the inner face looks white.

For a skincare carton with CMYK artwork outside and an unprinted white interior, C1S may be sufficient. A gift carton printed inside with photographic artwork or closely controlled brand colours is a stronger reason to evaluate C2S. Interior printing can sometimes be produced on an uncoated reverse, but the appearance may not match a coated face; test the actual design rather than assuming equivalence.

Smurfit Westrock offers Crescendo C1S and C2S grades, illustrating why the coating designation must be attached to a particular product specification. Neither designation, by itself, guarantees the required finished surface, stiffness or price.

SBS Paperboard Thickness: Points, Millimetres and GSM

Thickness, also called caliper, describes the physical distance between the two surfaces of a board. In packaging, one caliper point (pt) means 0.001 inch, or exactly 0.0254 mm. It is not the typographic point used for font sizes.

These are unit conversions, not recommendations for a specific product or claims about available SBS stock. A nominal 16 pt board is approximately 0.406 mm thick, but the mill specification and agreed measurement tolerances govern the material purchased.

Why GSM cannot be converted into a fixed thickness

GSM (g/m²) measures mass per square metre, not thickness. A dense board and a bulky board can have the same grammage but different caliper. Coatings, fibre composition and manufacturing method also influence the relationship. There is therefore no universal conversion such as “300 GSM equals 16 pt” for all SBS and FBB grades.

A real manufacturer's example makes the distinction clearer: the published specifications for MetsäBoard Pro FBB Go list one FBB option at 260 g/m² and 455 micrometres (0.455 mm). This is an example for that particular FBB product, not an SBS conversion chart or a value to assume for another mill.

When comparing board quotations, request the mill's actual grammage, caliper and tolerances together. Also ask for bending stiffness in the machine direction (MD) and cross direction (CD), and the grain direction proposed for the dieline. Two boards that match caliper can still perform differently when folded or loaded.

SBS vs FBB: Which Should You Choose?

Choose SBS when the specified white appearance, coated print surface and finished-package presentation justify it. Evaluate FBB when stiffness-to-weight efficiency is central. Neither is automatically the stronger, cheaper or better-printing option for every carton.

FBB stands for folding boxboard. Many FBB constructions have chemical-pulp outer layers surrounding a bulkier high-yield pulp middle layer. Metsä Board explains how that multilayer structure can provide useful stiffness at relatively low weight. The exact reverse shade and coating vary by FBB grade; do not assume that all FBB has a brown or uncoated back.

Consider two briefs. A small cosmetic carton needs fine pastel imagery outside and a clean visible cut edge: SBS C1S is a sensible grade to sample. A wider retail carton must keep a large panel flat while minimizing total board weight: include a coated FBB alternative and compare finished samples. In both cases, compare the same internal dimensions, closure, print target and packed product.

Ask which identified board grade passes your print, crease, fit and handling checks at an acceptable total cost. Do not accept an unapproved stock substitution because its GSM looks similar.

Printing and Finishing on SBS Paperboard

Printed SBS paperboard is often used for CMYK photography, fine typography, spot colours and decorative finishes. Its coated surface offers a controlled starting point, but the ink system and chosen surface treatments still determine the finished appearance.

  • CMYK printing: useful for photographs, gradients and multi-colour artwork. Check neutrals, small type and coverage on the specified board.
  • Pantone or other spot colours: provide a defined colour target, not an automatic perfect match on every substrate. Approve an agreed physical colour reference under controlled viewing conditions.
  • Hot foil stamping and embossing: can highlight a logo or texture. Confirm tool placement, fine-detail limits and any pressure-related marking on the reverse.
  • Spot UV, varnish or lamination: change appearance and surface performance. Evaluate adhesion, rub resistance, creasing and the consequences for recycling claims.

Smurfit Westrock's PrintKote portfolio identifies applications involving foil, embossing and spot UV, but that does not establish that every finish is suitable for every board or artwork combination.

A sample should answer more than “Does the colour look good?”

Use a structural blank to inspect fit, closure and panel stiffness, then assess a printed and finished sample on the intended production substrate when colour or finishing is critical. Check the assembled carton under realistic handling: inspect crease lines for cracking, glue areas for adhesion, high-coverage panels for scuffing and barcodes for readability. Record any approved material substitution before release.

A digital artwork proof can confirm content and positioning, but it cannot fully predict foil relief, gloss, the feel of a coating or the folding behaviour of a physical sheet. See VPN's custom printed paper box options for the related packaging applications.

When Should You Use SBS Packaging?

SBS is worth considering when the retail carton itself is part of the brand presentation. It is a candidate for cosmetic, skincare, perfume and other consumer-product cartons with demanding printed graphics. A compact folding carton can also protect labels and organise an already sealed product, provided the box passes fit and handling checks.

Choose the board with the full packaging system in mind. A heavy glass jar may need a fitted paperboard insert, a reinforced base or another box structure. A fragile product shipped through parcel networks may need a separate corrugated shipping box; choosing thicker SBS alone does not replace tested transport protection. If the package holds unpackaged food or another contact-sensitive product, the exact grade, coatings, inks and required compliance documentation must be reviewed for the destination market.

Before accepting a quote, ask the manufacturer to show the actual proposed board specification and what happens if that stock becomes unavailable. An alternative grade should be compared using the same critical attributes: coating side, whiteness where relevant, caliper, stiffness, grain, print quality, crease performance and contact requirements. A lower price is not a like-for-like saving if it changes the approved box.

Technical review point to document: on a production sample made from the identified stock, measure representative board thickness, fold along the proposed scores, inspect the cut edge and review the printed panel against the agreed reference. The person responsible for production or QC should record the measured results and approval date. Do not treat an unverified photograph or an unattributed quote as evidence of factory testing.

Is SBS Paperboard Recyclable?

Uncomplicated SBS paperboard is generally suitable for paper-fibre recycling, but the recyclability of a finished package must be assessed in its final construction and location. Mineral print coatings do not automatically make every paperboard carton unrecyclable; neither does a virgin-fibre starting material guarantee that a heavily modified carton will be accepted.

Plastic film lamination, barrier layers, windows, adhesive components, foil coverage and food residue can change processing and collection outcomes. Even when a fibre-based package can be repulped technically, a local collection programme may not accept it. How2Recycle advises checking local acceptance rather than applying one universal recycling instruction.

For B2B sourcing, request the complete material and finish specification, identify removable non-paper components, and verify disposal wording for the intended market before making a consumer-facing claim. “Made from SBS” and “recyclable everywhere” are not interchangeable statements.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a white carton automatically contain SBS paperboard?

No. Coated FBB and other white paperboards can look similar in a finished box. Confirm the mill grade or specification instead of identifying the material from a photo.

Can SBS replace corrugated board for shipping?

Not by default. SBS is frequently used for printed retail cartons; a corrugated shipper is designed around a different protective structure. Assess product fragility and real distribution conditions before selecting the outer pack.

Does C2S always give a better finished carton?

No. C2S is useful when coated printing is required on both faces. If only the outside is printed, C1S may meet the brief without an unnecessary reverse coating.

What should I approve before placing a bulk order?

Approve the exact board grade or documented equivalent, final dieline, physical fit, print colour reference, special finishes and a relevant finished sample. Record substitution rules in the purchase specification.
